The shared consent-UX principles these prototypes encode

  • Deny-by-default is the visual default. The resting state is closed/denied, shown calmly — safety is not alarming.
  • Refusing is one calm tap; opening carries friction. Leave, cancel, stay, and kill are always reachable; the affirmative "hand across / enter" action needs a deliberate press-and-hold.
  • The less-private side is visually unmistakable. Amber hazard chrome that never hides, on every surface that names the Google-Play / escape-hatch side.
  • The signed-consent moment shows exactly what will be signed (WYSIWYS): the world badge, the capability, the scope, the peer — derived from the pairing, so the far side can't spoof the clean side.
  • No padlock theater. Attestation appears only where it's true, and the honest qualifier travels with it.
  • The kill-switch never destroys the sealed base. It drops the span; the wallet, keys and image are preserved.
