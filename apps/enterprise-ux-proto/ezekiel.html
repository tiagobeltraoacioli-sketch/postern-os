What can this user see — honestly

Two mandatory controls decide it. Bell–LaPadula sets a clearance floor: no read up (you can't read data above your clearance) and no write down (you can't write below it, which would leak). Brewer–Nash raises Chinese walls between competitors: the first one you touch binds you, and its rivals go permanently barred. Everything fails closed.

3 · Chinese walls — touch binds, rivals bar

Open a volume in a conflict class and it binds the subject to that member; every competitor in the class then goes cryptographically unavailable — the key is simply never released. There is no override button; each binding is logged with a justification.
