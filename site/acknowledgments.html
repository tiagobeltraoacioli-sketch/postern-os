Homage · of the people, for the people

Nós, o Povo — We, the People

Three words open the Constitution of the United States — “We, the People” — and with them something new entered the world: authority that flows not from a crown or a conqueror, but from a people binding itself, in writing, to rules it agrees to keep even when keeping them costs something. That is the oldest and deepest idea I build on: a promise made durable, a pre-commitment that outlives the mood of the moment.

The Fifth Amendment is where that promise becomes a guarantee you can stand on: “No person shall … be deprived of life, liberty, or property, without due process of law; nor shall private property be taken for public use, without just compensation.” Read it slowly. The State is bound. Property is not held at the pleasure of power — it is secured by law, and by law that must follow process. From that security grew the freedom to contract and to enterprise, the freedom of atypical and innominate agreements, and, in our century, the freedom to write software and to issue tokens that no sovereign owns.

And liberty is not only the freedom to act in the open; it is the security of a private sphere. The right of the people to be secure in their persons, houses, papers, and effects — the Fourth Amendment's guarantee against unreasonable search and seizure — is the quiet precondition of every other freedom: there is no free conscience, no free enterprise, and no honest contract without a space the State cannot enter without cause and without process. This is why I build Postern. Privacy is not a feature to be bolted on; it is a fundamental right, and the tools that protect it are, at their best, instruments of ordered liberty — the private sphere defended by mathematics, as the Constitution defends it by law.

I do not treat these as abstractions. Constitutionalism, federalism, substantive due process of law, and the guarantees of life, liberty, and property are the load-bearing beams of the freer world we inherited. I honor the jurists who gave that order its voice — Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr. and Felix Frankfurter among them — and the long tradition of ordered liberty they served.

This is also the thread of my own scholarship. In The Cryptographic Constitution I argue that a protocol can do, by mathematics, what a constitution does by law: secure cooperation through strict pre-commitment and repeated games — civil cooperation without a sovereign. The ownerless Bloch base is that idea made to run: rules that bind because no one can bend them, the way the Fifth Amendment binds because a people chose to be bound. What America wrote in law, we try to keep faith with in code.

So Postern chooses its home, and says so plainly: Postern is and will be American — in its allegiance to these principles first, and in the ground it stands on. Not because it is fashionable, but because this is the constitutional order that made building at all possible, and gratitude should be spoken out loud.

It is, in the end, a beautiful story — the one called the United States of America: a people who wrote down their promises and then, imperfectly and across centuries, kept trying to honor them. Of the people, for the people.

God bless America. Deus abençoe a América — em profundo respeito, admiração e deferência à formação e à garantia dos direitos fundamentais da sociedade do século XXI.

— T.T., founder

With respect for the works & licenses we build upon

Postern is a builder among builders. We use others' work under their terms, name them, and never pretend their work is ours:

  • Android Open Source Project (AOSP) — used under the Apache License 2.0; attribution and NOTICE preserved. Postern products that run on Android are apps on stock Android; they are never called “Android,” nor a fork of it, nor “Postern OS.”
  • Google Play & Google Mobile Services (GMS) — Google Play Services are not redistributed by Postern; where a Google-enabled persona exists it is the user's own Google account and install, honestly labeled the less-private side (it phones home to Google — that is separation, not privacy). We do not circumvent SafetyNet / Play Integrity; we disclose attestation honestly instead. The “Android” and “Google Play” names appear only in nominative use to say what our software interoperates with, never to imply endorsement.
  • Anthropic — the Claude tooling used to build much of this. Where our apps talk to Anthropic, it is with the user's own key, and that path is labeled the least-private one.
  • The open-source ecosystem — the Rust, Nix, and web crates and tools we depend on, each under its own license, listed with the products that use them.

Everything here remains UNAUDITED, reference-prototype software; the coin is worthless by design and is not a security; the Bloch-SIS-PoW base is ownerless. None of the respect recorded above is a claim of endorsement by any party named.

Standing disclosures

The underlying protocol runs a mainnet beta — unaudited (audit contracted, not done), nascent, low-hashrate and 51%-attackable. The Module-SIS gate is now a difficulty-driven k-ramp (v0.1.0-alpha2): k=4 today, rising 5→6→7→8 as the network's own difficulty grows, easing back if hashrate falls — the Bitcoin model. The ramp is a structural filter, not a security claim: real security is cumulative SHAKE-256 hashcash work — hashrate × time. Nothing here is a certified security product yet.

The coin is not a security and not an asset. No token sale, no listing effort, no price. Disclosed in full: a 17% founder premine (10-year cliff, 40-year vest), structurally passive.

MIT / Apache-2.0, per crate — no copyleft embedded in the products.
