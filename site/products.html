Under the hood

Shared plumbing you don't install separately: Postern Core (the one canonical seal), Postern Quarantine (inbound-document safety — the mirror of Hygiene), Postern Tor (in-process Tor via Arti), Postern Telephony (carrier SMS/calls surface for Postern OS Mobile), the Postern SDK (now the single UniFFI setup_scaffolding! surface over the whole suite for Android / iOS / desktop — exactly-one-scaffolding, CI-checked; the app shells import postern_sdk directly; the SDK now exposes the real Argon2id KDF Rust-side, though the HTML app demos such as Chiave still use a PBKDF2 stand-in until they adopt it), postern-trail (the one append-only, SHAKE-256 hash-chained audit trail behind Yagabona and Ezekiel 1 — tamper-evident, not tamper-proof), and postern-policy (the one shared consent kernel — fail-closed decisions, required grant expiry, revocation-as-never-given, and the honest userland-not-kernel enforcement caveat every surface reuses). All built and test-verified; all unaudited. The mobile signer tracks the node's signed bytes per network; the byte-identity golden re-pin is still pending. The mobile↔desktop integration that turns the phone into an offline signer for the desktop (phone-as-signer) has a working v1 reference implementation (real interop) — QR + spoken-code pairing over a post-quantum-hybrid channel, a replay-proof sign-request protocol whose keys structurally cannot cross the wire, a WalletCore-backed mobile signer, and monotonic per-grant revoke (“revoke this laptop”). A v2 message shape is forward-declared but not built; everything is unaudited, and the desktop and phone ends are not yet unified on one wire. It is a reference of the shape, not a shipped feature.

AI interactions · bring your own key

Some apps talk to Claude — with your key, not ours.

A few of the apps use Claude to do their work. Sotto, Vedetta, and Tolmach need a key to function; Postern Store and Sarto take one only for an optional feature; Izbushka and Ezekiel 1 never touch the network at all. Wherever a key is used, you supply your own Anthropic (Claude) key and the app calls Anthropic directly from your browser — nothing is proxied through a Postern server (the one exception, Tolmach's subscription tier, is called out below). The AI path is optional; the parts of each app that don't need Claude work with no key at all.

how the AI concierge is wired

Bring your own key

bring your own key unaudited

How the AI works — and what leaves your machine. These apps talk to Claude using your own Anthropic API key. The key is entered in your browser and stored in this browser's localStorage; on the apps that offer it, you can seal the key at rest with a passphrase (AES-256-GCM), so the browser holds only ciphertext until you unlock it for a session. There is no Postern server in this path — nothing we run ever sees your key or your prompts.

But "your own key" is not "private AI," and it is not "local AI." Every message you send is transmitted from your browser directly to Anthropic (api.anthropic.com) and is processed on Anthropic's servers, under Anthropic's terms. Using your own key changes who is billed (you, on your Anthropic account) and who contracts with Anthropic (you) — it does not keep your prompt on your machine. Sending a prompt to Claude is sending your data to a third party. If a message would be unsafe for Anthropic to see, do not send it.

What this design does give you: no extra middleman between you and Anthropic, no Postern account, no Postern-side logging, and a single readable HTML file you can inspect to confirm the only place your prompt goes is api.anthropic.com.

The honest caveats

Voice input goes to a third party too — and it is a different third party. Voice uses your browser's built-in speech recognition (the Web Speech API). In Chrome and Chromium-based browsers this streams your microphone audio to Google's servers for transcription — it is not on-device, and this is the one data flow that does not go to Anthropic. It cannot be restricted by our page's network policy, because the browser performs it. Voice is off until you turn it on, is push-to-talk (the mic is live only while you hold the button — except Tolmach's Listen/Minutes modes, where the mic stays hot for as long as the mode runs), and we keep no recordings and add no telemetry. Once audio reaches Google it is handled under Google's terms, outside our control. Want nothing audio to leave the machine? Type instead — typed input stays local until you send it to Claude. Spoken replies are generated on your device.

Vedetta also talks to your market-data providers. To fetch quotes it connects to the providers you choose from a fixed allowlist (CoinGecko, Frankfurter, Finnhub, Yahoo Finance). Each quote lookup reveals the symbol you searched to that provider, and your Finnhub key is sent to Finnhub. So Vedetta's honest destination list is: Anthropic (your prompts), your chosen quote providers (your symbols), and — if you use voice — your browser's speech vendor (your audio). Never described as fully private.

Tolmach is bring-your-own-key only, for now. A hosted Subscription tier — where Claude usage would be billed to the operator instead of you, so your messages would pass through a Postern/operator proxy that sees the prompt text in transit — is planned as a future evolution, not enabled today. When it ships it will be clearly labelled the less-private option; the bring-your-own-key path will always remain and be the recommended one; and it will never be marketed as equally private.

Standing disclosures

The underlying protocol runs a mainnet beta — unaudited (audit contracted, not done), nascent, low-hashrate and 51%-attackable. The Module-SIS gate is now a difficulty-driven k-ramp (v0.1.0-alpha2): k=4 today, rising 5→6→7→8 as the network's own difficulty grows, easing back if hashrate falls — the Bitcoin model. The ramp is a structural filter, not a security claim: real security is cumulative SHAKE-256 hashcash work — hashrate × time. Nothing here is a certified security product yet.

The coin is not a security and not an asset. No token sale, no listing effort, no price. Disclosed in full: a 17% founder premine (10-year cliff, 40-year vest), structurally passive.

MIT / Apache-2.0, per crate — no copyleft embedded in the products.
