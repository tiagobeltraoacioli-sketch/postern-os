Disclaimer & legal

The full text, in plain language.

This page is the canonical version of every warning on this site. If anything elsewhere ever seems to promise more than this page does, this page wins.

1. Beta software — unaudited, no warranty

Everything Postern Labs publishes today is pre-production beta software, provided “as is”, without warranty of any kind, express or implied, including merchantability, fitness for a particular purpose, and non-infringement — as stated in the MIT and Apache-2.0 licenses that govern it.

No third-party security audit has been performed on any Postern product or on the underlying protocol. An audit is contracted but not done yet — until it is delivered, everything here remains unaudited. Internal review and passing test suites are not an audit. Nothing here is a certified security product.

2. Do not protect real secrets with it yet

This beta exists for evaluation: to be read, built, reproduced, and broken. It does not exist to protect anything you cannot afford to lose. Until the audits gate open:

  • do not store real passwords or secrets in the Vault;
  • do not send sensitive files through the Courier;
  • do not rely on the Messenger, Keys, Panic Lock, or any other component for real operational security;
  • do not treat the OS images or the desktop app as hardened production systems.

If your safety depends on your tools, use established, audited tools. We will tell you — on this page — when that calculus changes.

The AI features are the least-private path, by construction. The apps that talk to Claude do so with your own Anthropic key, calling api.anthropic.com directly — no Postern server is in that path, but the prompt is still processed by Anthropic, a third party, and voice input additionally goes to your browser's speech vendor (Google on Chrome/Chromium). Sending a prompt to a cloud AI is sending your data to a third party; do not send anything through it that would be unsafe for that third party to see. The exact destinations, the voice exception, and Tolmach's less-private subscription-proxy tier are laid out in full under AI interactions · bring your own key — that page governs; if anything here seems to promise more privacy, it wins.

3. The mainnet beta — what the label does and does not mean

The Bloch-SIS-PoW protocol now runs a mainnet beta. That is a designation, not a security claim: the network is live and persistent, and it is nascent, unaudited, and easy to attack. Precisely:

  • The fixed k=8 gate is retired — the gate is now a difficulty-driven k-ramp (node v0.1.0-alpha2). The earlier fixed k=8 hardening multiplied mining difficulty ~4096× with no security gain (β=q/16 is the trivial regime at any k) and choked block production at low hashrate. Under the ramp, k=4 applies today — below a future activation height, so every node upgrades first with no fork — then k climbs 5→6→7→8 as the network's own ASERT difficulty sustains above spaced thresholds, and eases back if hashrate falls (so block time stays ~30s). The ramp restores liveness; it adds no cryptographic hardness, and no security is claimed.
  • The security source is cumulative SHAKE-256 hashcash work — hashrate × time. The Module-SIS gate (at any k) is a structural filter that binds work to a lattice form — it is not the security source. The real hardness lever is a smaller β plus the outstanding audit, not k.
  • Very few nodes and low hashrate → 51%-attackable. A modest adversary could out-mine and reorganize this chain today. Do not treat confirmations as final in any way that matters.
  • Unaudited. A third-party audit is contracted but not done yet; the concrete-security (no-shortcut) analysis and the IACR ePrint publication for open expert review are still outstanding. “Mainnet beta” does not mean those gates have been passed — it means the chain is live while they are pursued.

Nothing on this site calls this network “secure” or “production”. If any other page ever seems to, this section wins.

4. The coin is not a security and not an asset

Do not attach value to the coin. The protocol's native coin exists as a consensus mechanism, not as an investment:

  • No token sale — past, present, or planned;
  • No listing effort, no market-making, no price — and no cooperation with anyone attempting to create one;
  • No promoter — the protocol is ownerless: no foundation, no curator, no official website (see Postern & Bloch);
  • No expectation of profit should be formed from anything Postern Labs says or sells — Postern's revenue is protection and support, never the token.

Coins on the mainnet beta carry no value claim: there is no price and no market, the network is 51%-attackable, and the difficulty-driven k-ramp (k=4 today) is a structural filter, not a security claim by itself. Not for investment. Use at your own risk.

5. The premine, disclosed in full

The single fact a skeptic will weigh against “ownerless”, kept visible on purpose: the protocol's genesis allocates a 17% founder premine, subject to a 10-year cliff and a 40-year vesting schedule, and structurally passive — it confers no governance power, no protocol privileges, and is backed by zero sale and zero listing effort. We state it here so nobody discovers it as a surprise.

6. Built vs designed — what actually runs

Labelled the same way everywhere on this site:

  • Built · tests pass — the Rust product cores (crates/postern-*) compile and their test suites pass;
  • Built · hosted + signed · QEMU boot pending — the Postern OS node ISO (x86_64): the image builds, is hosted and release-signed, but has not yet been observed to boot (no QEMU serial log or .evidence sidecar is published), so no boot claim is made and it is certainly not hardware-tested;
  • Built · hosted + signed · QEMU boot pending — the Postern OS desktop ISO: same status — it builds, is hosted and release-signed, and has not yet been observed to boot, so no boot claim is made;
  • Reference prototype — working front-ends (the self-contained web apps, the Tauri desktop app) that demonstrate the design; also the signed, published-but-never-device-booted “Postern for postmarketOS” apk packages — three signed apks in a live aarch64 repo, on an alpha build key (signed ≠ reproducible ≠ attestable);
  • Design / spec — documented plans (the Android container, the cloud and enterprise editions) that are not running software yet. The mobile OS image has moved past this: it is built, hosted, and release-signed, though not yet boot-tested.

If a claim isn't true yet, we phrase it as the plan — and a plan is all it is. The signed x86_64 ISOs and the aarch64 mobile image are hosted with checksums and minisign signatures, and three signed apks (the postmarketOS community edition) are hosted on a live aarch64 repo; building from source remains the strongest verification, and anything not yet hosted still installs from source.

8. This website

This site is static, self-contained, and makes no network requests: no analytics, no tracking, no external fonts, scripts, or images, and a Content-Security-Policy that forbids outbound connections. What you just read is everything it does.

Geo-restriction (HTTP 451). The hosted site returns HTTP 451 (Unavailable For Legal Reasons) to visitors it identifies as coming from jurisdictions under OFAC comprehensive sanctions. Be clear about what that is and is not: it is a hosting-service control only, applied at our edge to this website; it is a coarse IP-based measure that a VPN or Tor can evade, and it makes no claim to be exhaustive. Crucially, it does not — and cannot — gate the ownerless Bloch-SIS-PoW protocol (a permissionless peer-to-peer network with no operator) or the open-source software (MIT / Apache-2.0, buildable from source by anyone). It restricts who we serve this page to; it restricts nothing about the commons.

9. Pricing & how Postern Labs stays funded

Postern Labs is a company, and a company needs revenue — to keep building and to pay for the audit. We would rather disclose the plan now, plainly, than surprise you with it later. None of this is live yet, and there is no price on anything today. This is a forward-looking signal, for your awareness.

  • Mobile stays free forever; only Postern Desktop moves to freemium — and your data is never held hostage. Our published promise "personal editions stay free regardless" still holds for mobile: the mobile OS and every personal app on mobile are free forever — mobile is our free adoption channel and we are not touching it. The one thing that changes is Postern Desktop (the personal desktop suite), which now reserves the right to require a license in the future: each desktop install is free for 24 months, after which a personal key is required to keep receiving updates and premium features. We are saying this loudly, up front, because narrowing a published promise quietly would be exactly the dark pattern we refuse to run. We are doing it as fairly as we can: every desktop install gets 2 full years free, and every existing desktop install is grandfathered a full 2-year window from the changeover date — we are choosing to give 24 months of notice-and-grace, so no one is cut short. The key gates features and updates, never access to your own encrypted data: decrypt, read, export, and safety actions (Panic Lock) always work — key or not, expired or not; expiry fails open to a read-only grace mode, never a lockout. We still do not monetize the personal user's data: no selling or sharing your data, no telemetry, and no ads that track you — that promise stands. What is new, and we are stating it plainly: the free versions (all personal mobile apps, and Postern Desktop during its free window) may show advertising — but only the privacy-preserving kind: contextual, non-tracking ads chosen from nothing but the current screen or content, with no user profile, no advertising or device ID, no third-party ad SDK that phones home, no telemetry, and no data leaving your device, always clearly labeled as ads. Surveillance, programmatic, behavioral, and tracking-based advertising are forbidden — that is the only ad model consistent with Postern. Ads never appear in security-critical flows — Panic Lock, Vault decrypt/export, and backup are always ad-free — and the paid Postern Desktop key removes ads entirely (part of what the key buys). The coin is never monetized. The key is verified offline; we store only a salted hash and dates, never your identity. (None of this is live yet — there is no billing integration and no price today, the per-install free-window clock is not built, and no ad system is built, no ad network is integrated, and no ad placements ship; this is the announced model plus the offline-key mechanism, not a running payment or advertising system.)
  • Business & corporate use is free today. A company with a DUNS number can generate a free 6-month master license right now — unlimited users, which the master can then split into sub-accesses. The 6 months is the window while we structure the revenue model; after it, a license will be required for continued business/corporate use. A lighter business freemium tier (up to 3 desktops for 2 years) needs no DUNS. There is no live price today, any future charge is billed in fiat, never the coin, and the license is verified offline with no telemetry — we store only your DUNS and the dates to honor it. Personal mobile apps stay free forever; only Postern Desktop follows the freemium model above — 2 years free, then a key — and the coin is never involved in any of it.
  • Other intended revenue lines: priority / SLA support, deployment and signed/notarized build channels, attestation-verification services, and grants. These sell work and assurance, not your data.

The hard rule across all of it: revenue never comes from the coin (it is worthless and not a security), and never from selling or surveilling the personal user. Contextual, non-tracking advertising in the free versions is allowed because it takes nothing from you — no profile, no tracking, no data off your device; anything that would surveil or profile you is not. You pay Postern Labs (or view a labeled contextual ad) for protection, support, and provable integrity — never for a coin, and never with your privacy. This is not a commitment to any specific price or date, and it is not legal or financial advice.
